# Accounting

## Funding

FundingEscrow holds yvUSDC-1. Only the fixed Yearn manager can call `fund_round(amount)`, which queues two USDC payments without transferring anything immediately. The first is `amount // 2`, due at `(block.timestamp // 604800 + 1) * 604800`; the second is also `amount // 2`, due one week later. This matches proceeds' Thursday 00:00 UTC week boundaries. The round follows the scheduling transaction's inclusion week. An amount of at least two USDC base units ensures both payments are nonzero, and any rounding dust stays in the vault.

The only schedule state is `payments[2]` and `next_week`. `payments(0)` is the next amount, `payments(1)` the following amount, and `next_week` is zero when idle. A new round cannot overwrite an unpaid one. There is no permanent weekly payment, historical payment mapping, lifetime spending cap or link to proceeds eligibility. Yield and top-ups remain available for future rounds.

Anyone can call `work()` once a payment is due. It calls the fixed vault's `withdraw(amount, recipient, self, 0)`, which burns the required shares and sends exact USDC directly to the recipient with zero permitted loss. It then leaves the remaining payment at its original boundary; a delay never shifts the schedule. If both payments are overdue, each call pays one, so two calls settle them. Failed redemption rolls back the queue update and emits no payment. `canWork()` checks only that a payment exists and is due; it does not guarantee sufficient shares or vault liquidity. The keeper simulates `work()` before broadcasting.

The recipient defaults to fee burner `0xb911Fcce8D5AFCEc73E072653107260bb23C1eE8`. Only the manager can change it, and zero or the funding contract itself is rejected. Changes apply to unpaid instalments as well as later rounds. `assets()` reports the USDC value of vault shares, including yield and amounts scheduled but not yet paid. Loose USDC is recoverable but is neither used for payments nor counted as vault funding.

The manager and governance **ychad.eth** (`0xFEB4acf3df3cDEA7399794D0869ef76A6EfAff52`) can call `cancel()` or recover any ERC20 to themselves at any time. Recovering yvUSDC-1 automatically cancels all unpaid instalments, including on a partial recovery; recovering other tokens leaves the schedule unchanged. Failed recovery rolls back cancellation. Share recovery needs no vault redemption and does not depend on the proceeds contract. Already-paid USDC cannot be clawed back from the recipient.

## Claim-week settlement

ProceedsEscrow has no configured start or end dates, funding address or payment checks. Every week uses the designated Yearn voter's full weight on the gauge. This is the operating agreement, independent of whether payment has occurred; recovering funding does not stop proceeds. Early termination requires coordination and pausing sales while reconciling.

A sale uses `week = block.timestamp // 604800 * 604800`. There is no attempt to identify when each CRV unit was earned. The operating agreement is that Vyper and other authorised claimers leave claims and sales to this automation, which runs regularly and clears pending rewards near each week's end. Small carryover receives the following week's split. Missed periods and independent claims require operational reconciliation before normal sales resume; the contract does not detect those breaches.

`work(min_out, max_crv, sell_vyper)` calculates the execution week automatically. It captures that week's share if needed, calls the Curve minter, and pulls `amount = min(receiver_CRV_balance, max_crv)` from the Vyper multisig. Any remainder stays in the multisig for later batches, even when no new CRV is minted. All CRV in the receiver is treated as programme rewards under the operating agreement.

With `sell_vyper=True`, the fixed route exchanges the whole new batch for USDC and the contract splits the actual output. With `False`, it sells only `floor(amount * share / 1e18)` CRV for Yearn and leaves the remaining CRV in escrow for Vyper. All output from that smaller swap belongs to Yearn. Rounding goes to Vyper in both modes. A zero swap amount skips both pools and requires `min_out=0`. Existing escrow CRV, USDC and crvUSD are untouched by either mode.

All escrow CRV belongs to Vyper, including direct transfers. No reserve counter is needed: later work calls always swap from the new pull, never from the escrow's previous balance. Changing `sell_vyper` to `True` affects only the new batch. Vyper can withdraw its CRV to another address; withdrawing it to the gauge-receiving multisig makes it eligible for another pull and allocation. Use another recipient while automation is active, or revoke the allowance/end the programme before returning it there.

`yearn_receiver` starts as the proceeds contract itself. Yearn's USDC stays here unless management selects another nonzero address through `set_yearn_receiver`. Future sales then forward it atomically. Vyper's portion is either returned as USDC or retained as CRV, according to the mode. Receiver changes never sweep existing balances, and setting the receiver back to the contract resumes accumulation. A failed pull, swap, forwarding or return rolls back the entire call, claim and snapshot.

The fixed route is CRV → crvUSD through [TriCRV](https://etherscan.io/address/0x4eBdF703948ddCEA3B11f675B4D1Fba9d2414A14#code), indices 2 → 0, then crvUSD → USDC through the [USDC/crvUSD pool](https://etherscan.io/address/0x4DEcE678ceceb27446b35C672dC7d61F30bAD69E#code), indices 1 → 0. Both pool interfaces and assets are exercised on a mainnet fork. Only the fresh crvUSD returned by the first swap enters the second swap; existing crvUSD stays untouched. The second swap receives the operator's `min_out` unchanged, in **6-decimal USDC units**, protecting the complete atomic route. The first swap has a zero intermediate minimum; failure to meet the final USDC minimum rolls back both. There is no onchain price floor or minimum scaling.

Deployment records `DEPLOYED_WEEK` automatically and captures that week's share if the controller still has its effective vote. When the share is zero, the entire batch belongs to Vyper: converted to USDC or retained as CRV. A vote cast later in the same week affects the next week and cannot change an already captured snapshot. Calls always use their execution week, including the first call after rollover; `last_sale` controls only the cooldown. There is no automatic end date. Pause or stop automation and coordinate final settlement and handover when the programme ends.

## Weekly share

For each week, `checkpoint()` obtains:

```text
Yearn weight = effective_vote.slope * max(effective_vote.end - week, 0)
total weight = GaugeController.points_weight(gauge, week).bias
share = floor(Yearn weight * 1e18 / total weight)
```

A zero total requires zero Yearn weight and gives zero share. Division rounding goes to Vyper. Each week stores only a captured flag and its fixed-point share; the Snapshot event records both original weights.

The [Curve controller](https://github.com/curvefi/curve-dao-contracts/blob/master/contracts/GaugeController.vy) overwrites the latest voter record immediately when a vote changes, although the new vote takes effect next week. Capture before a revote. Public checkpointing is idempotent and works at the exact boundary, with no claim and while paused. Automatic capture requires `last_user_vote < week`. The manager can fill an unset snapshot through `set_missing_weight(week, weight)`; the contract reads the historical denominator and bounds the supplied weight. This is a trusted assertion, not a storage proof.

## Quotes and records

`claimable()` calls the gauge's `claimable_tokens_write` and returns existing receiver CRV plus pending rewards. Use eth_call so its checkpoint remains simulated. The separate view `quote(amount)` reads the pool quote and returns expected USDC (6 decimals). Zero input returns zero. Both functions are read at the same block by the tooling. Curve handles its own emission cap, annual reductions and checkpoint rounding. The escrow stores no accrual, inflation rate, emission cap, inventory, sale cursor or lifetime proceeds totals.

The keeper caps the pull at `min(claimable(), configured_max_crv)` and passes that amount as `max_crv`, with no multiplier or growth margin. `sell_vyper` defaults to true. When false, the keeper simulates `checkpoint()` at the same block and quotes only the Yearn portion of the capped batch. The configured slippage discount applies offchain to the resulting swap quote. A zero Yearn portion is retained with `min_out=0`. Additional rewards arriving before execution remain in the multisig. The configured cap defaults to 15,000 CRV. The minimum batch threshold is bypassed in the last five minutes before rollover, but the cap and cooldown still apply; drain a large backlog before that window. A delayed call crossing the boundary uses the new week's share, which can change the swap amount in retention mode; refresh quotes after rollover. There is no week parameter or deadline. A pending nonce must be mined or manually replaced before the bot can submit again.

Every successful `work()` records `last_sale`, including a batch retained without a swap. All keepers share a cooldown, initially 600 seconds; only the manager can change it, with a hard floor of 600 seconds. Changes apply relative to the last call without resetting that timestamp. A failed call does not consume the interval. `canWork()` returns whether work is unpaused and the interval has elapsed; it is independent of the caller and does not promise that a claim or swap will succeed. Checkpointing remains public during the wait. The keeper skips quoting until ready and can still checkpoint during cooldown. There is no exception to the cooldown at week boundaries.

Drawn and Sold events provide separate report ledgers. Each Drawn event records the USDC amount and recipient; reports derive total spending from these events and retain each payment's block timestamp and transaction. The dashboard shows the next scheduled funding payment separately. Actual payment timestamps remain independent of proceeds settlement rows. Weekly rows describe **claim-week settlements**, not precisely reconstructed earnings or a proof that every reward was collected. Reporting begins at the deployment week and warns when the current week lacks a snapshot. Each Sold event records USDC output, Yearn's amount and its receiver. Closed means the calendar week ended. Current balances come from token/vault reads. Yearn's total allocation, automatically forwarded USDC and management withdrawals are reported separately; an external receiver does not make forwarded amounts appear held in escrow.

The manager or governance (ychad.eth) releases held USDC or other non-CRV tokens with `withdraw(token, amount, recipient)`, including while paused. Either can pay TheDAO directly. **Only the Vyper multisig can withdraw CRV**, also while paused; it has no withdrawal authority over other tokens. Zero and the escrow itself are rejected as recipients. The `Withdrawn` event records the token, amount and recipient. Reports distinguish CRV and USDC withdrawals, and the dashboard shows Vyper's retained CRV balance when present. Each Sold event's `crv` is the amount actually swapped and `vyper_crv` is the amount newly retained; together they equal the pull. A withdrawal does not change `yearn_receiver` or previously recorded earnings. Neither escrow enforces grant milestones.
